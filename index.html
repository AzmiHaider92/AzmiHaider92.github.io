Azmi Haider

PhD in Computer Science from the University of Haifa, advised by Dr. Dan Rosenbaum. My research sits at the intersection of computer vision, generative modeling, and neural representations: learning compact representations of continuous signals, and building efficient methods for reconstruction and probabilistic inference from sparse or incomplete observations. My work spans tokenized neural fields and diffusion and flow-based generative models, with applications to 3D scene reconstruction, novel-view synthesis, physical fields, and image modeling [thesis].

I also hold an M.Sc. in Computer Science from the University of Haifa [thesis] and a B.Sc. in Electrical Engineering from the Technion. Alongside my studies, I worked at Intel on computer vision and deep learning, including 3D cameras, LiDAR, and face recognition.

Research Interests

  • Generative models for reconstruction: diffusion and flow-based models that reconstruct and generate images, depth, and 3D scenes from sparse or incomplete observations.
  • Representations of continuous signals: learning continuous and discrete (tokenized) representations that are compact, structured, and efficient to use.
  • Fast amortized inference: making probabilistic inference fast enough for real-world and large-scale use.
  • Deep learning for computer vision: representation learning and generative modeling for visual data.

I’m interested in research and applied roles in both industry and academia, particularly where generative modeling and computer vision are built into real products.

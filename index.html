About Me

I’m a researcher in generative AI and computer vision, with a PhD in Computer Science from the University of Haifa, advised by Dr. Dan Rosenbaum. I develop neural representations and efficient methods for reconstructing images, 3D scenes, and continuous signals from incomplete observations.

Previously, I worked at Intel on 3D cameras, LiDAR, and face recognition.

Neural Modeling of Continuous Signals: Representation, Reconstruction, and Probabilistic Inference.

Research focuses on learning compact representations of continuous signals and developing efficient methods for reconstruction and probabilistic inference from sparse or incomplete observations. Work spans token-based neural fields, diffusion and flow-based generative models, and applications including 3D scene reconstruction, novel-view synthesis, physical fields, and image modeling.

M.Sc. · Computer ScienceUniversity of Haifa · 2017–2020

Advisor: Prof. Hagit Hel-Or

Forgery Detection in Depth Images.

The thesis focused on understanding depth-camera noise and using it to detect forged depth data. The key idea was that a depth measurement contains both scene geometry and characteristics of the sensor that captured it, so manipulated data may appear geometrically plausible while exhibiting inconsistent noise patterns.
